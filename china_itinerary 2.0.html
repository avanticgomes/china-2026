Day by day

Twenty-three days.

Solo Shanghai weekend, group joins Sunday night, then together through ten stops. All inter-city travel by high-speed rail. Two side-day-trips bring back the original highlights — Wulong's UNESCO karst, and Huanglong's calcite terraces.

Week 1 — Shanghai & the imperial north
Sep 5 — 11
Day 1SAT · SEP 5
ShanghaiSolo · Arrive AM
Land PVG Saturday morning. Cab into the city, drop bags. Walk the French Concession, coffee at a quiet cafe, light lunch. Evening: Bund at sunset for the skyline across the Huangpu. Early dinner near the hotel.
Day 2SUN · SEP 6
Shanghai food & marketsSolo
Solo day, slow and food-focused. Morning xiaolongbao crawl through the back streets, a real wet market (not the Yu Garden snack street), afternoon coffee and dumplings in Tianzifang or Wukang Road. Easy on jet lag, perfect for moving at your own pace. Back to meet the group as they land Sun night.
Day 3MON · SEP 7
ShanghaiFirst full group day
The group's first day on the ground after their Sunday-night arrival. Yu Garden & old town in the morning, dim sum, walk along the Bund at sunset. The trip begins in earnest.
Day 4TUE · SEP 8
Suzhou half-day & chill ShanghaiHSR · 30 min each way
Late breakfast in Shanghai, mid-morning HSR to Suzhou. Humble Administrator's Garden (the most famous Ming-era scholar garden in China — a category of beauty nothing else on this trip delivers), lunch in the Pingjiang historic canal district, back to Shanghai by late afternoon. Evening: French Concession dinner, slow drinks.
Day 5WED · SEP 9
Shanghai → BeijingMorning G-train · 4.5h
Final Shanghai breakfast, then ~10:00 G-train from Hongqiao. Arrive Beijing South ~14:30. Drop bags, late lunch. Afternoon: Jingshan Park at sunset (the imperial view across the Forbidden City rooftops — open until 22:00). Evening: hutong wander through Nanluoguxiang, dinner somewhere local.
Day 6THU · SEP 10
Beijing
Forbidden City & Tiananmen — half a day inside the walls. Afternoon: Temple of Heaven (dawn is best, but afternoon works). Peking duck at Siji Minfu in the evening.
Day 7FRI · SEP 11
Great WallDay trip
Mutianyu or Jinshanling — never Badaling. A full day. Cable car up, toboggan or hike down. Back to Beijing for dinner.
Week 2 — Summer Palace, Xi'an, Wulong & Guizhou
Sep 12 — 19
Day 8SAT · SEP 12
Summer PalaceBeijing · Full day
The Qing-dynasty imperial retreat — Kunming Lake, the Long Corridor, the Marble Boat. Go early to avoid crowds. Pair with Yiheyuan dumplings nearby. Evening: a final hutong dinner, drinks at Houhai Lake.
Day 9SUN · SEP 13
Beijing → Xi'anRail · 4.5h
Mid-morning train. Sunset bike ride on the Xi'an city wall. Dinner in the Muslim Quarter — pull noodles, cumin lamb skewers.
Day 10MON · SEP 14
Terracotta Army
A full day at the warriors. Three pits, four to five hours. Hire an official guide on-site (~USD 20) — context transforms what you see.
Day 11TUE · SEP 15
Xi'an → ChongqingRail · 5.5h
Arrive into the most cinematic megacity in China. Hongya Cave lit up at night. Hotpot for dinner — yuanyang split if anyone can't handle the heat.
Day 12WED · SEP 16
Chongqing
Ciqikou old town, Yangtze cable car, the wild stacked-up cityscape. Evening: rooftop bar with a Yangtze view.
Day 13THU · SEP 17
Chongqing → WulongRail · 40 min – 1.5h
Early train from Chongqing North to Wulong South (HSR line opened July 2025), bus 45 min to the Visitor Center. Three Natural Bridges — the giant karst sinkholes from Transformers: Age of Extinction and Curse of the Golden Flower. Afternoon: Longshuixia Gorge (UNESCO karst). Overnight at Fairy Mountain Town.
Day 14FRI · SEP 18
Wulong → KailiPark AM · Rail PM
Morning: Fairy Mountain alpine grasslands (a complete change of scenery from yesterday's sinkholes). Brief stop at Furong Cave if time. Afternoon train onward — likely Wulong → Guiyang → Kaili, or back through Chongqing if the connection's better. Arrive Kaili late afternoon, hire a local driver for tomorrow.
Day 15SAT · SEP 19
Xijiang Miao VillageDrive · 1h from Kaili
Largest Miao settlement in the world, two thousand years old. Stay overnight inside the scenic area — the village lights up after the day-trippers leave. Wine ceremony at dinner is mandatory.
Week 3 — Sichuan, the alpine finale
Sep 20 — 27
Day 16SUN · SEP 20
Guizhou → ChengduRail · 4.5h via Kaili
Long rail day. Arrive Chengdu evening. Jinli or Kuanzhai Alley for dinner.
Day 17MON · SEP 21
Chengdu
Pandas at 7:30am sharp — they're active before the heat. Then People's Park tea house for an afternoon, Sichuan opera with face-changing in the evening.
Day 18TUE · SEP 22
Chengdu → JiuzhaigouRail · 2h + shuttle · 2h
Early morning train from Chengdu East to Huanglongjiuzhai (line opened Aug 2024 — far more reliable than flying to the mountain airport). Shuttle bus from the station to the park. Arrive afternoon, settle in, hydrate. Park sits at ~2,000m so altitude is gentle.
Day 19WED · SEP 23
Jiuzhaigou — south arm
First full park day. Cover the south arm of the Y-valley: Long Lake, Five-Color Pond, Pearl Shoal Falls. Enter at opening (7:00am) to beat the crowds, shuttle between the bigger jumps, walk the boardwalks between attractions.
Day 20THU · SEP 24
Jiuzhaigou — north arm
Second full park day, north arm — Mirror Lake, Nuorilang Falls, Pearl Lake, the Y-junction viewpoints. Autumn colors just starting to turn at this elevation.
Day 21FRI · SEP 25
HuanglongDrive · 2h each way
Day trip from Jiuzhaigou base. Calcite terraced pools — alien, otherworldly, the trip's most photographed landscape. Highest altitude day (3,500–3,900m at the upper viewpoints). Cable car up, walk down. Don't push if anyone's feeling the altitude.
Day 22SAT · SEP 26
Jiuzhaigou → ChengduShuttle + rail · ~4h
Slow morning, last look at the park if you want. Afternoon shuttle (~2h) and rail (~2h) back to Chengdu. From Chengdu East, taxi straight to Tianfu Airport. Final hotpot somewhere on the way if there's time. Be at TFU by ~22:30.
Day 23SUN · SEP 27
Chengdu → SydneyDeparts 01:35 · Direct
Sichuan Airlines 3U3891 direct to Sydney. Land SYD ~14:00 Sunday afternoon. Full day to recover, back at work Monday.
Apps

Get your phone ready before you fly.

China runs on its own digital ecosystem. Google, WhatsApp, Instagram, Gmail, YouTube — all blocked. Download and register everything below before leaving Sydney; some apps need verification that's tricky to do once you're inside the Great Firewall.

Two things to do first. Install a VPN on every device before you fly — VPN websites are blocked once you arrive. And buy an eSIM (Airalo, Nomad, or MobiMatter all work in China) — many include built-in VPN access, so Google Maps, Gmail, and WhatsApp keep working over mobile data even when hotel Wi-Fi can't reach them.
Essential — install before you fly
01
Alipay 支付宝
Payments · everywhere
The default way to pay in China — restaurants, taxis, attraction tickets, even street vendors. Cash is technically legal but rarely accepted. Register with the "Tour Pass" feature using a foreign card before you fly. Note: small fee on transactions over ¥200.
02
WeChat 微信
Messaging + everything else
WhatsApp on steroids. Messaging, second mobile wallet, social feed, business cards, ride-hailing — all in one app. Set it up with your real name & phone number ahead of time; verification can be glitchy. Revolut and HSBC cards work; some neobanks don't.
03
Didi 滴滴出行
Ride-hailing
China's Uber. English interface, accepts foreign cards, in-app translation with drivers. Drivers will sometimes message asking for a selfie so they can spot you in a crowd — that's normal. Hailing a regular cab without it is nearly impossible.
04
Amap 高德地图  or Apple Maps
Navigation
Google Maps doesn't work in China and hasn't been updated since 2010 — places will be off. Amap has an English interface. Apple Maps works fine if you're on iOS. Get one of these; both is safer.
05
Pleco
Translation · offline
The best Chinese-English dictionary app, works offline. Camera mode for menus and signs. Better than Google Translate for Chinese specifically — and unlike Google Translate, doesn't require a working VPN connection.
06
Google Translate
Translation · backup
Type, speak, or point your camera at text. Works in China but only over mobile data via your eSIM — not over hotel Wi-Fi. Pair it with Pleco for a belt-and-braces approach.
07
Trip.com
Trains, flights, hotels
Formerly Ctrip. The most foreigner-friendly way to book China's high-speed rail and domestic flights. English interface, 24/7 English support. You'll use this constantly given your eight rail legs.
08
A VPN
Access blocked sites
For your laptop and as backup on phones. ExpressVPN, NordVPN, or Surfshark are reliable. Install before you arrive — VPN provider websites are blocked inside China.
Nice to have
09
Dianping 大众点评
Local restaurant reviews
Chinese Yelp. The way locals find restaurants, especially in food-obsessed cities like Chengdu and Chongqing. Use Google Lens or your camera translation to read reviews.
10
Meituan 美团
Food delivery, attractions
Uber Eats meets Groupon. Order food, book tickets, find local discounts on spas and experiences. Useful for longer hotel stays and lazy nights in.
11
Xiaohongshu 小红书
Hidden gems, trends
Pinterest meets Instagram meets TripAdvisor — but localised. Where Chinese travelers share real recommendations. Best app for finding hole-in-the-wall spots in Chengdu, Beijing's hutongs, or anywhere off the main tourist drag.
12
Google Lens
Camera translation
Point your phone at a menu or sign for instant translation. Best for printed text; less reliable on handwriting. Lives inside the Google app — needs working data + VPN access.
Booking timeline

When to book what.

China runs on real-name reservations and 15-day advance windows. Some things you book now; some you can't book until two weeks before. Here's the order.

The big news first: Australian passport holders don't need a visa until December 31, 2026 — 30 days visa-free for tourism. Confirm your passport has at least 6 months validity past Sep 27, 2026 (so issue date no later than ~March 2026). Beyond that, no embassy queue, no application form, no fees.

By end of May 2026
International flights
SYD → PVG (Fri Sep 4 eve) and TFU → SYD (Sun Sep 27, 01:35 dep.). Long-haul Asia flights from Sydney are typically cheapest 2–6 months out. Past mid-July, Sep prices climb sharply. Book through Qantas, China Eastern, Juneyao directly, or Trip.com for combined fares.
Book now

Xijiang Miao Village (limited inside-scenic-area beds), Jiuzhaigou (Zhangzha town hotels fill in autumn), Chengdu (Jinli district). Most are free-cancel up to 1–7 days before — book early, cancel if plans change. Trip.com and Booking.com both work; Trip.com has better local inventory.

All 6 group members need passports with 6+ months validity past Sep 27 (i.e., valid until at least end of March 2027). Anyone whose passport expires before April 2027 should renew now — Australian renewals take 3–6 weeks.
Check now
Travel insurance
Especially important for the Jiuzhaigou + Huanglong stretch (altitude, remote area, mountain weather). Make sure your policy covers China and includes medical evacuation. Cheaper booked early.

Field notes

Things to remember.

A few logistics worth nailing down well before September.

Mind the altitude
Jiuzhaigou park is ~2,000m. Huanglong's upper viewpoints reach 3,900m — that's where altitude sickness hits some people. Take the cable car up, walk down. Drink water aggressively. Pack mild altitude medication (acetazolamide) as a precaution. Don't drink alcohol the night before Huanglong day.
You're well clear of Golden Week
National Day Golden Week starts Oct 1. Your trip ends Sep 27, with 4 days of buffer — no overlap with the chaos. But booking pressure for trains and hotels builds steadily through September, so finalize everything by mid-July.
Group can extend at the end
Your redeye out of Chengdu lands Sunday afternoon — full day to recover, back at work Monday. The rest of the group can either fly home with you, or book a separate return from Shanghai/Beijing with extra days to wind down. Easier to manage when international tickets are booked individually rather than as a group fare.
